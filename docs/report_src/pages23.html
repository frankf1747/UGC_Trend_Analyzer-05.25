01 — How it works

What happens between
the review and the answer

Nothing here is a prompt to a chatbot. Four models do four narrow jobs, each one checkable on its own, and every judgement call is frozen to a file that lives in version control.

Stage one — build a taxonomy from the data, not from a workshop

Split into sentences

4,173 reviews → 9,954 sentences

A mixed review stops being one verdict and becomes several separate claims.

→

Embed each sentence

BGE-small-en-v1.5 · 384-dim · CLS pooled, L2-normalised · local GPU

Meaning becomes geometry, so sentences saying the same thing land near each other.

→

Cluster to discover themes

spherical k-means · k=24 chosen by silhouette

Density clustering was tried first and rejected: 2 clusters, 90% noise.

→

Name once, then freeze

taxonomy.json · 16 aspects · git-tracked

An LLM reads cluster exemplars and writes the taxonomy one time. Labels are never regenerated at runtime, so a theme cannot silently change meaning.

↓
Stage two — measure every sentence against that fixed taxonomy

Assign themes

DeBERTa-v3 zero-shot NLI · 9,954 × 16 = 159,264 entailment passes · multi-label, τ=0.50

Each sentence is tested against each written definition. It can match several aspects, or none — 20.7% match nothing and are excluded rather than forced.

+

Score polarity

RoBERTa (review-tuned) · continuous −1 … +1

Per sentence, never inherited from the star rating. This is the step that recovers complaints buried inside positive reviews.

→

Weight by outcome

OLS · review-level · category + year fixed effects · HC1 robust SE

Ranks aspects by association with the rating, with confidence intervals — not by how loud they are.

→

Extract language

weighted log-odds · informative Dirichlet prior

Which words genuinely distinguish praise from complaint. TF-IDF cannot answer this.

Audited, not asserted

102 sentences judged by hand: 88.2% theme accuracy (CI 80.6–93.1), 95.1% polarity. Two earlier designs scored 56.2% and 82.4% and are documented.

Claims checked mechanically

Every number in every generated brief is parsed and matched to its evidence before it renders. 103 claims, 0 unmatched — and it has caught real errors.

Runs anywhere

No API key, no vendor, no per-call cost. Every model is open-weight and runs on a laptop GPU; outputs are committed so the dashboard opens instantly.

02 — What it found, and where else it applies

The score tells you who left.
The text tells you why.

Four findings from this corpus that a rating-based read cannot produce — then what the same method is for.

17.5%
Complaints hide in five-star reviews
Of all sentences inside 5★ reviews, 17.5% are complaints — 26.5% in 4★. These are problems reported by customers who stayed, and a rating-weighted read counts every one of them as praise.
+0.22★
Some complaints are demand signals
Complaining you cannot find a product travels with a rating 0.22★ higher (p=0.03). Scarcity complaints come from devotees. Route them to distribution, not to the fix list.
84.6%
The worst category is a logistics problem
Protein snacks rate lowest (3.88★). Its condition-on-arrival mentions are 84.6% negative, at 14% of reviews — complaints about dates, bags and descriptions. Nothing in the recipe needed changing.
“natural”
A marketing claim that backfires
The single strongest marker of a low-rated drink review. Reviewers reach for the word while giving one star — the claim was read as a promise about taste and missed it. In pet food the equivalent word is “china”.
The same method, wherever text sits beside a score
Live comment streams
Comments arrive faster than anyone can read them. Aspect plus polarity per comment turns a feed into a live count of which specific thing people are reacting to — a pricing backlash separated from a delivery outage within minutes, not at the next review cycle.
Support & CSAT
Ticket text inherits the survey score, so friction inside “satisfied” tickets is never counted. Same correction; weight against renewal instead of the survey.
App store & marketplace
A five-star review naming a missing feature is the best product input a team receives, and exactly what a star-weighted dashboard discards.
Surveys & complaints
eNPS, patient experience, regulated narratives. Aspects rather than moods means “discharge communication” can be tracked as improving or worsening over time.
Silent mislabelling
A classifier that never abstains files chatter under a real theme. Guard: an explicit unassigned class — 20.7% here — and a hand-judged accuracy published beside every result.
False causation
Polarity is read from the same text that produced the score, so the link is mechanical. A triage order, never “fix X and the rating rises”.
Thin slices
Small segments produce confident nonsense. Guard: floors that make the system decline to answer — four of fourteen categories report no language analysis at all.
Drift & fluent prose
Renamed themes destroy trends; generated summaries read well whether or not they are right. Guard: taxonomy frozen in git, and every number machine-checked against its evidence.

To point it at something else you need free text, a numeric outcome, and a few thousand rows. Only the taxonomy is rewritten — an afternoon's work — and every stage after it is unchanged: same embeddings, same entailment, same regression, same claim checker. It runs on open weights, on one laptop GPU, with no API key and no per-call cost.

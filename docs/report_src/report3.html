01 — Approach

Read every sentence,
not every rating

Most feedback systems attach a review's star rating to all of its text. That single shortcut is where the useful signal gets destroyed — so the pipeline scores each sentence independently, then rebuilds the picture from the bottom up.

Three approaches were tried and discarded before this one
Rejected · density clustering

HDBSCAN returned 2 clusters and left 90% of sentences as noise. Keeping the conventional choice would have meant discarding nine tenths of the evidence.

Rejected · clusters as labels

56.2% accurate. Clustering must place every sentence somewhere, so unrelated chatter lands inside a theme and stays there.

Rejected · definitions as prompts

62.5% left unassigned. Long compound hypotheses entail badly; short label phrases fixed it with no change of model or data.

The result of doing it this way: 17.5% of sentences inside five-star reviews turn out to be complaints — criticism from customers who stayed, which a rating-based read cannot see at all. Two earlier versions of the assignment step scored 56.2% and 82.4%; both failures are documented rather than dropped.

02 — Application

Any text with
a score attached

Nothing here is specific to food, to Amazon, or to stars. Wherever an organisation collects free-text feedback next to a numeric outcome, the same shortcut is being taken — and the same buried signal is waiting.

SaaS support & CSAT

Friction described inside "satisfied" tickets is never counted. Aspect extraction surfaces it and ranks it against renewal, not against the survey score.

App store reviews

A five-star review naming a missing feature is the best product input a team gets, and exactly what a star-weighted dashboard discards.

Employee engagement

Comments paired with eNPS, same inheritance bug. Aspect themes let "manager support" be tracked as improving or worsening — which "sentiment down 4%" never tells you.

Patient experience

Complaints buried in high-scoring surveys — discharge instructions, waiting communication — are operational and cheap, precisely because the patient was satisfied.

Financial complaints

Regulated narratives need triage, not sentiment. A published error rate and an explicit "unassigned" class is what makes such a model auditable.

Hospitality & travel

"Loved the hotel, the air conditioning was broken" — one review, two facts. Multi-label assignment keeps both instead of averaging them away.

How to tell whether your own data has this problem
Test one

Pull fifty pieces of feedback with the highest scores and read them properly. Count how many name a specific problem. If the answer is not near zero, your dashboard is not showing it to you.

Test two

Ask what your top theme would be if you ranked by impact on the outcome rather than by mention count. If nobody can answer, you are prioritising by volume.

Test three

Ask what your classifier's accuracy is, on a sample somebody actually checked. An unanswered question here means every number downstream is unmeasured.

What a new domain needs: free text, a numeric outcome, and a few thousand responses. Only the taxonomy is rewritten — an afternoon's work; every stage after it is unchanged. It runs on a laptop GPU with no API cost. What it will not give you: causation — polarity is read from the same text that produced the score, so the ranking is a triage order, not a treatment effect.
